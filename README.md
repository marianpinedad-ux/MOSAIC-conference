# MOSAIC-conference
MOSAIC (Molecular Occupancy and Structural Analysis of Interacting Contacts) is a Python-based computational tool designed to characterize molecular interactions between residues across a structural trajectory.

Using a protein structure and contact matrices generated by RING, MOSAIC converts interaction data into quantitative metrics, comparative tables, and graphical representations. These outputs make it possible to investigate how contacts are organized and distributed, quantify their occupancy, and examine how residue–residue contacts vary throughout the analyzed trajectory.

# The program combines two complementary approaches:
1. Configurable main analysis: supports the study of intramolecular or intermolecular contacts according to the selected chains and interaction types.
2. Target protein–peptide analysis: independently characterizes interactions between the chain defined as the target and the selected peptide chains.
Together, these approaches provide both an overall view of the system and a focused characterization of the molecular interface of interest.

# What can MOSAIC help investigate?
MOSAIC is designed to help answer questions such as:
- Which residue pairs are detected in a larger proportion of the trajectory?
- Which interactions show the highest occupancy?
- Which residue pairs participate in more than one type of interaction?
- Which regions of the target protein concentrate contacts with high occupancy?
- How are interactions distributed among the different peptides?
- Which peptides show greater coverage of residues identified as hotspots?
- Which candidates should be prioritized for structural inspection or further analysis?
The results help organize and prioritize structural information in a reproducible way, supporting the comparison and interpretation of different systems or conditions.

# Main capabilities
# 1. Molecular interaction analysis
MOSAIC processes three interaction categories:
Category	Description
VDW	Contacts associated with van der Waals interactions and residue proximity.
HBOND	Hydrogen bonds between donor and acceptor groups.
SALTBRIDGE	Ionic interactions or salt bridges between oppositely charged groups.


These categories can be analyzed individually or combined to provide an integrated description of residue contacts.
# 2. Occupancy quantification
One of the core metrics is occupancy, which represents the fraction of trajectory frames in which a given interaction is detected.

This metric makes it possible to distinguish between:
- Occasional contacts, detected in a small proportion of the trajectory.
- Recurrent contacts, detected across multiple frames.
- High-occupancy contacts, detected in a large proportion of the analyzed frames.
Occupancy is calculated for each residue pair and for each interaction category.
# 3. Integrated total occupancy
MOSAIC calculates an integrated metric that considers whether at least one of the selected interaction types is present for a given residue pair.

When two or more interaction categories occur simultaneously in the same frame for the same pair, that frame is counted only once.

This approach makes it possible to evaluate the overall occupancy of a contact without overestimating it because of the coexistence of different interaction types.
# 4. Interaction diversity
The program quantifies how many interaction types reach the occupancy threshold defined for each residue pair.

This metric helps distinguish between:
- Residue pairs supported mainly by a single interaction category.
- Residue pairs exhibiting a combination of different interaction categories.
- Residue pairs showing a broader variety of contact mechanisms.
Occupancy and diversity should be interpreted together: a contact may have high occupancy but low diversity, or it may combine substantial occupancy with a wider variety of interaction types.
# 5. Identification of priority contacts
MOSAIC generates complete tables and candidate rankings based primarily on integrated total occupancy and, as a complementary criterion, interaction diversity.

This makes it easier to identify residue pairs with the most prominent contact patterns within the analyzed dataset.

The program also generates visualizations relating these two metrics to support comparisons between candidates.
# 6. Target protein characterization
The target-specific module examines intermolecular interactions between the chain selected as the target and the chains identified as peptides.

This analysis runs independently of the filters used in the main analysis and includes all three interaction categories.

# It provides:
- Number of detected residue pairs.
- Integrated occupancy and occupancy for each interaction type.
- Comparative summaries of VDW, HBOND, and SALTBRIDGE interactions.
- Distribution of contacts among peptide chains.
- Ranking of target protein residues.
- Identification of target residues with high occupancy values.
- Occupancy breakdown by target residue, peptide residue, and interaction type.
- Changes in the number of contacting residue pairs throughout the trajectory.
# 7. Exploratory hotspot detection
MOSAIC includes an operational criterion for identifying potential hotspots. In the program, hotspots are defined as target protein residues whose maximum integrated occupancy reaches or exceeds 50%.

Based on this selection, the program:
- Identifies target residues that meet the criterion.
- Determines which peptides contact those residues.
- Calculates the number of hotspots contacted by each peptide.
- Estimates hotspot coverage for each peptide chain.
- Compares the mean and maximum occupancy of contacts involving those residues.
- Generates a comparative peptide ranking based on hotspot coverage and contact occupancy.
This evaluation provides a quantitative criterion for prioritizing candidates and guiding subsequent studies. Hotspot identification is descriptive and depends on the selected threshold; by itself, it does not demonstrate binding affinity or functional relevance.
Scientific visualizations
The program generates figures to facilitate the exploration and interpretation of results.
Visualization	Information provided
Integrated occupancy vs. diversity	Compares residue-pair occupancy with the number of interaction types detected.
Heatmaps	Show contact occupancy for each interaction category and for the integrated metric.
Target residue ranking	Highlights target residues with the highest contact occupancy.
Frame-by-frame dynamics	Shows how the number of unique residue pairs fluctuates throughout the trajectory.

Figures are exported as PNG files at a configurable resolution, allowing them to be incorporated into reports, presentations, and publications after scientific review and interpretation.
Generated results and files
At the end of the execution, MOSAIC organizes the results in an output folder and creates a ZIP archive for convenient download and storage.

The output package includes:

- Complete main-analysis table.
- Top 20 ranking from the main analysis.
- Complete table of target protein residue pairs.
- Top 20 ranking of target–peptide pairs.
- Interaction-category summary.
- Interaction summary by peptide chain.
- Target protein residue ranking.
- Occupancy breakdown by residue, pair, and interaction type.
- Frame-by-frame contact dynamics record.
- Peptide ranking based on interactions with hotspots.
# Figures
- Integrated occupancy and diversity plot for the main analysis.
- Target occupancy and diversity plots.
- Heatmaps for each interaction category.
- Integrated occupancy heatmap.
- Graphical ranking of target residues.
- Frame-by-frame contact curve.
- Peptide comparison against hotspots.
# Traceability and documentation
- Audit of residue correspondence between the PDB structure and RING labels.
- Summary of identified residues by chain.
- Record of residues present in the PDB but absent from the RING matrices.
- JSON file containing the parameters and selections used.
- Text file with a preliminary, guidance-oriented interpretation of the results.
- Text file describing the contents of the results folder.
Tables are exported as CSV files, figures as PNG files, and parameters as JSON. All outputs are bundled into a ZIP archive.
# General workflow
1. Data preparation: obtain the PDB structure and the corresponding contact matrices for the same trajectory using RING.
2. File upload: provide the PDB file and the three interaction matrices.
3. Validation: check file integrity and the correspondence between residue labels.
4. System definition: assign the biological meaning of the chains and identify the target protein and peptides.
5. Configuration: select the scope of the main analysis and the interaction categories.
6. Processing: calculate occupancy and diversity metrics.
7. Target characterization: analyze target–peptide contacts and identify potential hotspots.
8. Interpretation: review the tables, rankings, and graphical representations.
9. Export: download the results package for further analysis, comparison, or documentation.
Requirements and input data
Required files
Four files are required to run the analysis:
File	Purpose
.pdb structure	Provides structural identification of residues and their chains.
.cm_VDW matrix	Contains van der Waals contact information.
.cm_HBOND matrix	Contains hydrogen-bond information.
.cm_IONIC matrix	Contains ionic interaction or salt-bridge information.


All four files must correspond to the same molecular system and trajectory.
Execution environment
MOSAIC is implemented as a Python notebook and is designed to run in Google Colab. It can also be executed in a local Jupyter environment.

# It uses the following tools and libraries:
- Python
- NumPy
- pandas
- Matplotlib
- Seaborn
- IPython
- Google Colab (for direct file upload and download in that environment)

# How to run MOSAIC
# Option 1. Google Colab
1. Download MOSAIC.ipynb from this repository.
2. Open Google Colab.
3. Select File → Upload notebook and upload the notebook.
4. Run the cells in order, from beginning to end.
5. When prompted, upload the PDB file and the three RING matrices.
6. Complete the requested selections and biological chain assignments.
7. Review the generated tables, rankings, and figures.
8. Download the ZIP archive containing the results.
# Option 2. Local Jupyter environment
1. Install Python and Jupyter Notebook or JupyterLab.
2. Install the required dependencies:
pip install numpy pandas matplotlib seaborn jupyter
3. Download MOSAIC.ipynb and place the four input files in the same directory.
4. Start Jupyter:
# jupyter notebook
5. Open the notebook and run the cells in order.
6. When the analysis is complete, locate the resultados_ring folder and the generated ZIP archive.

# Configurable parameters
The analysis includes centralized parameters that allow users to adapt the selection criteria and presentation of results.
Parameter	Default value	Purpose
Minimum occupancy threshold	0.05 (5%)	Criterion used to calculate interaction diversity.
Number of candidates	20	Number of pairs included in the main rankings.
Maximum number of pairs in plots	40	Limits the number of candidates displayed to improve readability.
Figure resolution	300 dpi	Defines the image export quality.
Output folder	resultados_ring	Directory where results are stored.


The hotspot threshold used by the target interpretation module is defined in the code as 0.50 (50%).

Parameters can be modified to adapt the analysis. When comparing different systems, it is recommended to use equivalent criteria and document any changes.
Quality control and reproducibility
MOSAIC includes checks designed to improve processing consistency:
- Verifies that input files are present and readable.
- Checks that matrices are square and maintain consistent dimensions and labels across frames.
- Verifies agreement between the labels of the three interaction categories.
- Compares residue identification between the PDB structure and the RING matrices.
- Reports correspondence discrepancies instead of silently assigning contacts by position or shifting indices.
- Avoids counting the same pair twice because of matrix symmetry.
- Integrates interaction categories without counting the same pair more than once in a given frame.
- Retains the parameters and selections used to document each run.
These checks help identify correspondence or formatting problems before the results are interpreted.
Scientific scope and considerations
MOSAIC provides descriptive metrics for characterizing the organization and occupancy of molecular contacts throughout a trajectory.

The following points should be considered:
- High occupancy indicates that a contact is detected in a substantial proportion of the analyzed frames.
- Diversity indicates how many interaction categories exceed the selected threshold; it does not directly represent the energetic strength of a contact.
- Integrated occupancy is neither an energy value nor a direct measure of binding affinity.
- A hotspot identified according to the program's criterion is a candidate based on contact occupancy, not an experimentally validated site.
- Greater hotspot coverage by a peptide does not, by itself, demonstrate that the peptide has higher binding affinity.
- Interpretation should take into account structural quality, the trajectory, the contact-generation method, and the biological context.
Therefore, the results should be used as a tool for characterization, comparison, and prioritization. They should be complemented by energetic, structural, experimental, or functional analyses when required by the scientific question.
Potential applications
MOSAIC can support:
- Characterization of protein–peptide interfaces.
- Comparison of different peptides interacting with the same target protein.
- Identification of regions and residues with high occupancy values.
- Exploration of interaction patterns in molecular complexes.
- Prioritization of residues for further structural studies.
- Descriptive comparison of molecular trajectories or systems.
- Preparation of tables and figures for scientific reports, theses, and presentations.
These applications describe potential uses of the metrics calculated by the program and do not imply automatic validation of molecular mechanisms.
